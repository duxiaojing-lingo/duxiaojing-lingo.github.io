✦ Entry — Summer, Year 2

Hello there! I'm Xiaojing — a phonetician and PhD researcher at the Cambridge Phonetics Laboratory, University of Cambridge. I spend my days farming acoustic data, measuring how voices shimmer and shift: from the tones of Jin Chinese to the subtle breathiness hiding in everyday English speech.

Before arriving in this valley, I studied at Wake Forest University, earning a B.S. in Mathematical Statistics & Linguistics in 2024 — where I first discovered that language and mathematics could grow side by side in the same field.

These days I tend two main plots: a frame-level voice quality classifier that teaches machines to hear what phoneticians hear, and the tones of Huojia Jin Chinese, a dialect with secrets buried deep in its mountain terroir. It's honest work, and the harvest has been good.

MAIN QUEST · 2025 · Under Review

Phonation in Jin Chinese: A Frame-Level Voice Quality Analysis

Du, X., Post, B., & Nolan, F.

Submitted to Journal of Phonetics

The first study to apply frame-level voice quality probes (trained on SSL features) to Jin Chinese tones. Reveals that creaky phonation is not merely paralinguistic but tonally contrastive in this under-studied Sinitic variety.

MAIN QUEST · 2024

Automatic Frame-Level Voice Quality Labelling Using Self-Supervised Speech Representations

Du, X., Post, B., & Nolan, F.

Interspeech 2024, Kos, Greece

Introduced a frame-level VQ labelling pipeline using SSL models (wav2vec 2.0, HuBERT) fine-tuned on annotated corpora. Demonstrated that mid-network layers carry the richest phonation-type signal — a key finding for interpretable voice AI.

SIDE QUEST · 2025

Tonal Perception and the Huojia Minimal Pair: Listener Sensitivity to Phonation Cues

Du, X., Post, B., & Nolan, F.

ICPhS 2027 (in preparation)

A perception experiment using resynthesised Huojia Jin minimal pairs to isolate which acoustic dimensions (F0, VQ, duration) listeners use to distinguish tones. Directly tests Lu et al.'s theoretical claims with Cambridge participants.
